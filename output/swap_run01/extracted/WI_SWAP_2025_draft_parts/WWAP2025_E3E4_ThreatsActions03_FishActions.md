## Introduction and Overview

This is a summary of threats or issues affecting the conservation of fish SGCN and actions that can be implemented at the source, or to address the effects of the source on the species or its habitat. Threats and actions are organized using the Conservation Measures Partnership classification. 
1
 Distinguishing the source of the impact from the effects or the changes that occur to the species and its habitat is important because the two typically need a different approach and set of conservation actions. Conservation actions may be focused at the source of the activity, which is related to the location, type and extent of the development or the action can be focused on restoring or replacing habitat elsewhere or at the edge of the development. Multiple sources of impact may have the same or similar effects on species or habitat. Similar effects may be addressed collectively by a single action or suite of actions.
If the threat/issue and its associated conservation action(s) apply to one or multiple species they are identified as such. Conservation actions overlapping in content or scope may be grouped.
In addition to threats and actions discussed in this report, many lakes and rivers such as the Great Lakes 2 and the Mississippi River 3 have identified priority conservation goals and actions that address the threats facing the fish community.
More about how threats and issues or conservation actions were developed and how this information can be used to make conservation decisions can be found in E3-4 Threats & Actions of the 2025 WWAP collection. The most frequently cited issue category for fish SGCN is water quality alteration, which can be divided into three subcategories of nutrient loads, sediments and to a lesser extent chemical pesticides/herbicides. All three categories enter aquatic systems via point and non-point sources from certain agricultural practices and development within the watershed. The agricultural practice sources tend to predominate in the southern half of the state. Development areas affecting SGCN fish tend to be more dominant in the northern part of the state and along the Great Lakes.

The most cited action categories to address sources of pollution are comprehensive management to protect, preserve and restore aquatic habitat on private and public lands; compliance with water quality regulations and standards; rigorous application of industry or sector standards or practices (e.g., agricultural best management practices); and integrated pest management that relies on biological or natural method of pest control rather than chemical methods. Wake enhancement, the intentional creation of large wakes for recreational purposes, poses a significant threat to fish and their habitats. Enhanced wakes contribute to shoreline erosion, disturb critical spawning and nursery areas, and degrade water quality by increasing sediment resuspension. This activity can also damage aquatic vegetation important for fish shelter and breeding. Restore habitat for the longear sunfish in the rivers and lakes where they occur, with emphasis on improving water clarity.

## Threat/Issue F2-Shoreline Development and Alteration

Protection and restoration of natural shorelines are essential to protect fish habitat from the impacts of wake enhancement. This includes maintaining existing native vegetation, reestablishing riparian and aquatic plant communities, and implement actions to stabilize banks and dissipate wave energy. The second category is policies and regulations that maintain, encourage and support protection of these natural communities. Local policy and regulations are relatively more effective in this respect because they can more readily target aquatic systems that provide SGCN habitat.
Follow best practices to improve wildlife habitat and water quality, such as the ones identified by the Healthy Lakes and Rivers program that focus on planting with native plants and reducing and eliminating runoff.

## Dams and Water Management/Use

Natural system modification in aquatic habitats occurs through hydrologic control and infrastructure, including the many lock and dam structures for commercial navigation along the Mississippi River and water management along the Wisconsin River. These modifications alter water flow and depth that affect habitat for SGCN fish. Tributaries to these rivers and other river aquatic systems are also affected by water level management throughout the state.
