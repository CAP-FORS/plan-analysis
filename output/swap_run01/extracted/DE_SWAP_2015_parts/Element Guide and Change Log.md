## DEWAP -3 ELEMENT GUIDE/CHANGE LOG

The Delaware Department of Natural Resources and Environmental Control (DNREC) Division of Fish and Wildlife (DFW) has prepared this guide as a road map to Delaware's Wildlife Action Plan (DEWAP) for the Regional Review Team and others to readily find sections that address each of the eight required Elements.
It is combined with the change log for efficiency and ease of review. The reader is therefore directed to the location of all aspects of each element in the document at the same time as being informed of any changes from the original 2006 Delaware Wildlife Action Plan.

## Delaware's First Revision-Summary of Updates and Improvements

With this DEWAP revision, Delaware presents its updates and improvements from the 2006 DEWAP for each of the eight Elements. It begins with a reassessment of Delaware's conservation targets: Species of Greatest Conservation Need (SGCN) and key habitats. It reassesses threats affecting these fish and wildlife SGCN and their habitats using an International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN)/Wildlife Tracking and Reporting Actions for the Conservation of Species (TRACS)-compatible approach. It applies Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies (AFWA) Best Practices and United States Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) Guidance to make the plan compatible at the regional and national levels. This revision presents the results of an updated process to identify and prioritize conservation actions to address or alleviate these threats, again applying the IUCN/TRACS-compatible approach through engagement of multiple partners and stakeholders. It describes plans for monitoring, reviewing and revising the conservation targets and the effectiveness of the plan. Finally, it outlines the partner, stakeholder, and public input process used during the two-year revision process.
The goal of the DEWAP is to provide direction for Delaware's conservation efforts for SGCN and their key habitats over the next decade. It represents a vision and a strategy that DNREC DFW developed and shares with its partners for the conservation of fish and wildlife in the state.

## Example of the DEWAP Eight Element Flow

Following a conservation planning approach, the DEWAP process proceeded through each of the eight required elements sequentially and incorporated the guidance provided by the USFWS and AFWA Best Practices. DNREC DFW staff, five taxa committees (mammals, birds, amphibians and reptiles, fishes, and invertebrates) and multiple key partners assessed the abundance and distribution of fauna in Delaware, identifying SGCN based on available scientific information (Element 1). The red knot, for example, was identified as a SGCN following an evaluation of its status, abundance and distribution in the state, and existing conservation efforts that have identified it as a species of highest conservation concern. The rufa subspecies of red knot is federally listed as Threatened and globally and state ranked (by NatureServe) as G4T2 and S1M. Furthermore, the IUCN classifies the red knot as Least Concern (decreasing), and the USFWS ranks it as Tier 1a (Immediate Action Needed) on its proposed list of priority bird species occurring in the region. The DEWAP Birds taxa team used the DEWAP criteria (consistent with the Northeast Lexicon) and ranked the red knot as Tier 1, Highest Conservation Concern, so that status was adopted in the DEWAP.
Following its proposal as a SGCN, the public was invited to provide comments (as with all SGCN and key habitats) through the DNREC website (Element 8).
The red knot principally inhabits the Unvegetated Sandy Beach and Intertidal Mud and Sand Flat community types identified in this DEWAP (Element 2). These dynamic coastal habitats are relatively rare and vulnerable in Delaware (Element 2). Delaware's red knot population, as well as populations of several other migratory shorebirds, is threatened by prey loss (horseshoe crabs), pollution, predation by pets and subsidized predators, habitat loss and degradation (e.g., climate change, shoreline development), disturbance, and the need for better species specific information (Element 3). Needed conservation actions to address these threats were then determined and include protection of degraded habitat and maintenance of existing habitat through climate change scenario planning, horseshoe crab population conservation, population monitoring (including banding, surveys, etc.), and encouraging the public and private landowners to participate in Delaware's coastal protection programs, such as the Delaware Bayshore Initiative (Element 4). A priority monitoring need for this SGCN is to determine population status and trends on Delaware's coast (Element 5). The anticipated products of fulfilling this monitoring need are data to fill information gaps to assess the status and/or condition of red knots and inclusion of this species in the DNREC DFW's DEWAP database (Element 5). These data will also contribute to regional and national conservation efforts in which Delaware is a partner, such as the USFWS Red Knot Recovery Plan, Western Hemisphere Shorebird Reserve Network (WHSRN), and the North American Shorebird Conservation Plan (Element 7).
The conservation and/or increase of populations of the red knot through habitat protection, restoration and/or enhancement was identified as a priority conservation action (Element 4). By monitoring the Element Guide and Change Log DEWAP -5 implementation and degree of success of these conservation actions, DNREC DFW and its partners will be able to quantify the performance measures for each, including population numbers, the number of programs, and the number of acres restored, enhanced, and/or protected by climate change planning scenarios including providing for habitat shifting (Element 5).
The adaptive management approach will allow DNREC DFW to not only quantify these performance measures, but also compare the monitoring results to infer whether the conservation actions are increasing the number and weights of red knots each year. If the status and condition of red knots shows no significant improvement, then the conservation actions can be modified to intensify habitat and prey protection measures or target key areas and cooperative projects with partners if funds limit the expansion of the conservation measures (Elements 1, 3, 5 and 7). In this way, a feedback loop between monitoring, conservation actions and management objectives will be maintained (Elements 1-5).
Detailed information on the selection of SGCN, key habitats, conservation actions and more are summarized throughout the DEWAP and its appendices. An overarching change was the inclusion and application of the following guidance documents: AFWA Best Practices (AFWA, BPWG 2012); State Wildlife Grants Effectiveness Measures (AFWA 2012); National and Regional Service Guidance; and the key regional projects: Regional Conservation Need (RCN) projects, the Northeast Synthesis and Lexicon for each of the Eight required Elements

## Changes, Improvements and Updates Since 2006

Chapter 3 provides updated threats to SGCN and their habitats in Delaware. Some of these threats are global or national, while others are regional, statewide, or local. They include threats or problems that stress wildlife species or their habitat as well as management challenges to address the threats. The Northeast Lexicon and Synthesis were incorporated and applied to update this Element, which develop criteria and support the process of identifying and prioritizing this Element. Specifically, the Northeast Lexicon was applied to code all threats which cross-walked threats identified in the TRACS system and includes the action drivers or needs that complement the IUCN system. Appendix 3 provides the compiled list of all threats identified from key plans and from the input gathered from a series of four statewide workshops of partners, stakeholders and the public.
Threats affecting SGCN or their habitats in Delaware were determined using the 2006 DEWAP list as a foundation that was supplemented by a review of the best available information from over 100 existing conservation programs and plans (Appendix 1). Climate change is an example of a threat where more updated information was incorporated into the threats determination, assessment and ranking process for this revision.
To provide consistency in identifying threats to SGCN and key habitats, the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) recommended by the AFWA Best Practices, as incorporated into the TRACS System Action Drivers (Appendices 3 & 4) was adopted for use in this DEWAP. "Threats" or "Issues" are defined as "the proximate human activities or processes that have caused, are causing, or may cause the destruction, degradation, and/or impairment of biodiversity targets." Action Drivers (or needs) were also included.
To identify threats to SGCN and their key habitats (Element 3), all original sources used in the original 2006 DEWAP were reviewed, along with a number of more current reports and updates. Through a series of workshops, teams of experts, partners, and stakeholders reviewed the latest information on threats to where more updated information was incorporated into the threats determination, assessment and ranking process. Teams then grouped and condensed these threats, where similar, for species suites, habitat associations, or broader taxa applicability. A similar process was conducted for identifying and updating threats to each key habitat. Habitat threats were also grouped and condensed to higher tier habitat groupings whenever possible to reduce redundancy and highlight common threats.

## Changes, Improvements and Updates Since 2006

Chapter 4 presents hundreds of individual inventory/research/monitoring needs and conservation actions.
An updated and expanded process was conducted to identify those conservation actions that would best address the threats and problems identified in Chapter 3 and protect the SGCN and key habitats discussed in Chapters 1 and 2 respectively. Where information was insufficient to identify conservation actions, the process focused on identifying research, inventory, and monitoring needs to obtain such missing information, identifying priorities and tangible products to fill these information gaps. Involving key partners and stakeholders in identifying and prioritizing conservation actions was vital for creating a DEWAP that can be implemented by federal, state and local governments, non-governmental organizations, universities and other partners.
The process of identifying actions began with reviewing the 2006 DEWAP and finding updated actions through a comprehensive review of existing international, national, regional, state, and local conservation and management plans (Appendix 1.D). Once these actions were compiled, they were reviewed by the DEWAP Technical Review Team. Actions were refined by the Technical Review Team, Stakeholders, and Partners through a series of workshops and further consultation with staff. This allowed taxa experts and partners to develop a draft list of priority actions that most effectively addressed the identified high priority threats and captured the priorities repeated in partners' plans. Partners and stakeholders were all engaged in prioritizing actions for this revision.
For the 2015 DEWAP, actions were organized by standard classification system (IUCN/TRACS, as recommended in the Northeast Lexicon), and used as a foundational reference to develop a matrix of actions that addressed each threat identified for Delaware's target species and habitats. Actions were developed for the highest priority threats identified. This served as the first order of prioritization. Each action developed was also assigned a rank of 1-3 (1= Highest, 2= Higher, and 3=High), as described in Appendix 3 and 4. They applied the criteria to represent the degree of urgency and likelihood of success in the priority rank for each SGCN and habitat actions. These scores were summed for relative priority scores for each action.
All actions presented in this document are considered important. Those actions that have a greater conservation effect across taxa and habitats were considered higher priority and are presented first as statewide, overarching actions. The broader taxa level actions that address a broader suite of species and habitats, ecological and habitat groups respectively, present the next level of priority. The finer filter habitat and species level priority actions are then presented under each specific habitat within the habitat section.
Individual habitat or community lists were generated where additional specific actions were deemed necessary for that community. Even more specific actions necessary for individual species or a small group of wildlife species were included under the ecological group to which those species belong.

## Changes, Improvements and Updates Since 2006

The 2015 DEWAP presents a rigorous monitoring and adaptive management framework that will be used to assess the status of SGCN and habitats as well as monitor the effectiveness of DEWAP conservation actions (Chapter 5). Delaware's approach identifies existing monitoring efforts and tools currently used by DNREC DNREC DFW or its partners to assess SGCN, key habitats and related issues, as listed in the plans and programs in Appendix 5. If monitoring is not identified for an SGCN or species group/taxa, Chapter 4 of this DEWAP describes monitoring actions for other species which occupy the same habitats; these recommendations are prioritized to benefit the overall habitat, community, or assemblage, including many other SGCN. In cases where not enough information exists to monitor a species or group, or for which monitoring protocols have not yet been developed, this need is documented and followed by a conservation action intended to address that information need. This is true for some taxa groups such as small mammals and invertebrate groups for which standardized protocols need to be developed, and where baseline data do not exist to form the basis of a monitoring protocol. In these cases, these overarching taxa needs are described in Chapter 1 under the appropriate taxa.

## Changes, Improvements and Updates Since 2006

Chapter 5 describes the process and the timeframe by which the DEWAP will be reviewed and updated. Full revision will be completed in ten years (2025). Interim sections will be reviewed and revised throughout the ten-year period. The goal is to keep the document as up-to-date as possible. As gaps in the data that exists in 2015 are filled, new ones will be identified. Knowledge of the status of Delaware's wildlife and the conditions that affect wildlife management are constantly changing. Thus, the current DEWAP is seen as a dynamic document.
Annual measures for reporting accomplishments are conducted through TRACS and Federal Aid project reporting. Results that indicate any changes or new information, including information from periodic review by the Taxa Committees, will be integrated into the DEWAP by the Technical Team.
Perhaps the most efficient and effective outcome of this plan will result from the SGCN, key habitats and priority conservation actions being integrated into the revision processes of DNREC DFW and its many partners' plans and programs (as listed in Appendices 1.D and 5). This integration effort alone produces the ripple effect for conservation efforts across the state, providing a consistent and unified approach for conservation of Delaware's wildlife.
