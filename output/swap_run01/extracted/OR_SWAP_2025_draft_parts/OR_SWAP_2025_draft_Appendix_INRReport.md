This analysis suggests that there was minimal change in the amount of dune habitat between 1855 -1910 and 1998, and a 24% loss in coastal dunes by 2016. 54% of the area mapped as Coastal Dunes in 2016 was also mapped as Coastal Dune in 1998, and 38% of the 1855 -1910 dune area was also mapped as coastal dunes in 2016.

The minimal gain (3%) by 1998 reported here differs in magnitude from the 18% increase reported in

The Coast GLO map coastal dune polygons are reasonably large, so they likely include small patches of vegetation, but these are likely balanced by non-dune historic map classes that likely have inclusions of open sand. This analysis suggests a loss of 24% of the historic coastal dunes between 1910 and 2016. While some of this is due to offsets in locating the narrow coastal beaches, there do appear to have been significant shifts in where open dunes are located, with some dunes now stabilized and open sand dunes established where vegetation was previously stabilized.

The 1998 vegetation map didn't separate out the many small patches of Oak Forest within the agricultural matrix of the Willamette Valley the way that the 1981 and 2016 maps were able to, so the apparent collapse of Oak Woodlands by 1998 is likely an overestimate, and the apparent partial rebound in 2016 is likely an artifact rather than a true pattern.
